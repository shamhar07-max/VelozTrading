Understanding Leverage
Leverage lets you control a larger position with a smaller amount of your own capital. For example, with 1:100 leverage you can open a $10,000 Gold trade using only $100 of your own funds. Profits and losses are both magnified, so leverage should always be used with a clear risk plan.
Example — Gold at 1:100 Leverage
You deposit $100. Leverage of 1:100 gives you $10,000 in market exposure. If Gold rises 1%, your position gains $100 — a 100% return on your $100. If Gold falls 1%, your position loses $100. This is why Stop Loss orders matter.

Protecting Your Position
SL
Stop Loss
Your trade closes automatically if the market moves against you by a set amount. This limits your maximum loss on every trade. Always recommended.
TP
Take Profit
Your trade closes automatically when it reaches your profit target — locking in gains even if you are not watching the screen.
MC
Margin Call & Stop-Out
If your account equity drops to 100% of required margin you receive a margin call. Positions close automatically at 50% margin level to protect against negative balances.
Real Trade Example — EUR/USD
You believe the Euro will strengthen against the Dollar. You open a Buy (Long) on EUR/USD at 1.0850 with a lot size of 0.10 using 1:100 leverage. You set a Stop Loss at 1.0820 (30 pip risk = ~$30 max loss) and a Take Profit at 1.0910 (60 pip target = ~$60 potential gain). If the market hits your Take Profit, the trade closes automatically with your profit banked.
